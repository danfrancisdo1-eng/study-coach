Domain 1: Identify the business value of generative AI solutions (37.5%)

Foundational concepts

  • Generative vs other AI: Generative AI creates new content (text, images, code, summaries) from patterns learned in training. Predictive or classic machine learning scores, classifies or forecasts from structured data.
  • Pretrained models: Models already trained on broad data, ready to use with prompting. They are the fastest and cheapest route to value for general tasks.
  • Fine-tuned models: A pretrained model further trained on your own domain examples to shift its style or specialist behaviour. It costs more effort than prompting or grounding and needs quality training data.
  • Tokens: The units of text a model reads and writes. Usage and cost scale with the number of tokens in prompts and responses, so long prompts and long outputs cost more.
  • Cost drivers: Model choice, token volume, how often it is called, and any fine-tuning or hosting. Matching model size to the task keeps cost down.

Business value and ROI

  • Choose a solution to fit need: Start from the business problem, then select the lightest tool that solves it: an out-of-the-box Copilot, a low-code agent, or a custom build in Foundry.
  • Return on investment: Compare measurable benefits (time saved, quality, revenue, satisfaction) against total cost: licences, usage, data preparation, training and change management.
  • Value of machine learning: Machine learning finds patterns in data to predict and automate. Its value depends on a lifecycle of data, training, deployment, monitoring and improvement.

Limits and risks

  • Fabrications: Models can produce fluent but false content (also called hallucinations). Reduce them with grounding, clear prompts and human review.
  • Reliability: Output can vary between runs and is not guaranteed correct. Important decisions need verification and human oversight.
  • Bias: Training data can carry unfair patterns that appear in outputs. Mitigate by testing, diverse data and responsible AI review.
  • Data quality: AI results are only as good as the data behind them. Poor, outdated or ungoverned data gives poor answers.
  • Secure AI: Protect data, access and outputs. Keep Copilot inside existing permissions and compliance controls, and guard against oversharing.

Prompting and grounding

  • Prompt engineering: Shaping the instruction to get better output: state the goal, give context, specify format and audience, and iterate.
  • Grounding: Supplying relevant, trusted data (your documents, emails, business data) so answers are tied to facts rather than only to training.
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation: RAG retrieves relevant content at question time and gives it to the model with the prompt. It brings current, organisation-specific knowledge without retraining.

Remember it

Fine-tuning vs grounding (RAG): Fine-tuning changes how the model BEHAVES; grounding changes what the model can SEE when it answers.

Pretrained vs fine-tuned: Pretrained is off the shelf; fine-tuned is tailored with your own examples, at higher effort.

Fabrication vs bias: Fabrication is made-up facts; bias is unfair skew. Both need human review, but the causes differ.

Generative vs predictive AI: Generative CREATES new content; predictive FORECASTS or CLASSIFIES from data.

A new hire: the pretrained model is a graduate with general knowledge, fine-tuning is months of job training, grounding is handing them the company handbook at the desk, and the prompt is your instruction. Chant: Pretrained knows. Fine-tuned trained. Grounded looks it up.

Domain 2: Identify benefits, capabilities, and opportunities for Microsoft's AI apps and services (37.5%)

Microsoft 365 Copilot

  • Copilot Chat and apps: Microsoft 365 Copilot works in apps such as Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams and in chat, using your work data within your existing permissions.
  • Researcher agent: Performs deep, multi-source research across the web and your work data and returns a cited report. Pick it for market scans and cited analysis.
  • Analyst agent: Works through data to find insights, such as analysing spreadsheets and numbers. Pick it for data analysis rather than broad research.
  • Agents in Copilot: Agents extend Copilot for specific tasks or processes. Prebuilt ones cover common needs and organisations can build their own.

Building and extending

  • Copilot Studio: Low-code tool to build and manage custom agents and copilots, connect them to data and actions, and publish them to channels.
  • Agent Builder: Simple, guided way for business users to create an agent inside Microsoft 365 Copilot without code.
  • Microsoft Foundry: Microsoft's platform for building AI apps and agents with models and tools. It is the renamed Azure AI Foundry, announced at Ignite in November 2025.
  • Foundry Tools: The prebuilt AI capabilities (such as vision, speech and language) that developers add to applications. The outline names Foundry Tools alongside Microsoft 365 Copilot.

Matching service to need

  • Out-of-the-box first: For common productivity gains, use Microsoft 365 Copilot as is. Move to Copilot Studio for custom business processes, and to Foundry for custom AI solutions needing developers.
  • Opportunities by function: Look for repetitive, content-heavy or data-heavy work: summarising meetings, drafting, analysis, customer service and research.
  • Naming changes: Azure AI Foundry is now Microsoft Foundry. Answer options may use either name, so recognise both.

Remember it

Researcher vs Analyst: Researcher reads the world (web plus files, cited report); Analyst reads the spreadsheet (data insights).

Copilot Studio vs Foundry: Studio is low-code for business builders; Foundry is the developer platform for custom AI apps.

Agent Builder vs Copilot Studio: Agent Builder is the quick start inside Copilot; Studio is the fuller tool for managing custom agents.

Azure AI Foundry vs Microsoft Foundry: Same platform, new name.

A kitchen: Microsoft 365 Copilot is the ready meal, Copilot Studio is the meal kit you assemble, Foundry is the professional kitchen. Chant: Ready meal. Meal kit. Pro kitchen.

Domain 3: Identify an implementation and adoption strategy (22.5%)

Responsible AI and governance

  • Responsible AI principles: Microsoft's principles are fairness, reliability and safety, privacy and security, inclusiveness, transparency and accountability.
  • AI council: A cross-functional group that guides AI strategy, prioritises use cases and provides oversight.
  • Meeting standards: Set policies, review use cases for risk, and keep humans accountable for AI-assisted decisions.

Adoption and change

  • Adoption team: A dedicated group that plans rollout, training and measurement of AI use.
  • AI champions program: Enthusiastic employees who model good use, support peers and gather feedback to spread adoption.
  • Barriers to adoption: Common blockers are fear of job impact, lack of skills, unclear value, trust concerns and data readiness. Address them with communication, training and visible quick wins.

Impacts and licensing

  • Data impacts: Copilot respects existing permissions, so clean up oversharing and data quality before rollout.
  • Security and privacy: Apply existing security, compliance and privacy controls to AI, and be clear what data is used.
  • Cost and licensing: Choose between Copilot licences per user and Azure AI consumption-based pricing according to the scenario. Pilot, then scale on measured value.

Remember it

AI council vs AI champions: The council GOVERNS from the top (strategy, priorities, oversight); champions ADVOCATE from the floor (peer support).

Adoption team vs AI council: The adoption team runs the rollout; the council sets direction and oversight.

Per-user licence vs consumption pricing: Copilot is licensed per user; Azure AI usage typically scales with what you consume.

Exam Tips & Common Pitfalls

  • Read the scenario for who the builder is: business user means Copilot Studio or Agent Builder; developers mean Foundry.
  • Researcher is for cited, multi-source research; Analyst is for data and spreadsheets.
  • Fabrications are reduced by grounding and human review, not by hoping for a better model.
  • Grounding or RAG beats fine-tuning when the need is current company data.
  • Answers that skip governance, permissions or human oversight are usually traps.
  • Recognise both Azure AI Foundry and Microsoft Foundry; they are the same platform.
  • Always connect cost to tokens and usage, and value to measurable business outcomes.
  • Pace yourself: you have 45 minutes, so do not stall on one question.
  • Choose the simplest tool that meets the need before a custom build.
